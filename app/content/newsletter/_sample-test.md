:::highlight Sit down. You are not in trouble.

Almost everybody fails that question, and it is not for lack of revision. She was never on the syllabus.

So I am not marking you down. I am marking down whoever set the papers you sat at school.

Here is the answer in full, the way it should have been taught.

:::photo

She was a doctor first. A surgeon, in fact, trained in obstetrics and gynaecology, and a lecturer at Makerere University Medical School. Then in 1994 she became Vice President of Uganda, and she stayed until 2003. No woman on this continent had held that office before her.

She pushed for women's rights in law, in education and in health, and she kept coming back to the mothers who were not surviving childbirth. She knew first-hand what poor healthcare does to women.

And when the politics ended, she went back to public health, to work on HIV/AIDS. The highest office an African woman had ever reached, and she treated it like a longer ward round.
