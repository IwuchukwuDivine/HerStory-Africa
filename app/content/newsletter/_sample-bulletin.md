In 1994 a Ugandan doctor named Specioza Wandira-Kazibwe became Vice President of her country. No woman anywhere in Africa had held that office before her.

But the office is the least interesting thing about her. Before the title there was the operating theatre.

She trained as a doctor and surgeon, specialised in obstetrics and gynaecology, and lectured at Makerere University Medical School, one of the most respected in East Africa.

:::column

## A surgeon in the room

So think about who actually walked into that office. A surgeon, from a country where women were dying in childbirth at alarming rates, who knew first-hand what poor healthcare does to women's lives.

She used her years in office the way you would expect a doctor to. She pushed for women's rights in law, in education and in health, and she kept coming back to the mothers who were not surviving childbirth.
