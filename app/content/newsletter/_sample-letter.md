I have a history question for you, and I want you to answer it __before__ you read any further.

:::highlight Who was the first woman to become vice president of an African country?

Take your time. I'll wait.

If the name that came to you was not __Specioza Wandira-Kazibwe__, you are exactly why I am writing.

I am enclosing her photograph, so you can put a face to the name.

:::photo

She was a doctor first. A surgeon, in fact, trained in obstetrics and gynaecology, and a lecturer at Makerere University Medical School. Then in 1994 she became Vice President of Uganda, and she stayed until 2003. No woman on this continent had held that office before her.

What stays with me is what she did with it. She pushed for women's rights in law, in education and in health, and she kept coming back to the mothers who were not surviving childbirth. She knew first-hand what poor healthcare does to women.

And when the politics ended, she went back to public health, to work on HIV/AIDS. The highest office an African woman had ever reached, and she treated it __like a longer ward round__.

I have put everything I could find about her in one place. [You can read her full story here.](https://herstoryafrica.com.ng/women/specioza-wandira-kazibwe)

Write back and tell me who you guessed.
