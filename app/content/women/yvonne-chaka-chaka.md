## Biography

Yvonne Ntombizodwa Machaka was born on 18 March 1965 in Dobsonville, Soweto, to a Swazi mother and a Pedi father. Her father, a musician, died when she was eleven; her mother, a domestic worker, raised three daughters on roughly forty rand a month. At sixteen she became the first Black child to appear on South African television, and in 1984, at nineteen, she was discovered by Phil Hollis of Dephon Records in Johannesburg. Her debut album "I'm Burning Up" (1986) and the follow-ups "Sangoma" and "Thank You Mr. DJ" (1987) made her a continental sensation, her joyful "bubblegum" pop crossing borders that apartheid politics could not close. Her signature hit "Umqombothi" later opened the film Hotel Rwanda, and a 1990 tour earned her the title she still carries: the Princess of Africa.

Fame never ended her education. She earned a Bachelor's degree in Speech and Drama from Trinity College, London in 1997, along with diplomas in Adult Education and in Local Government, Management and Administration from UNISA, where she also taught literacy part-time. She has performed for Queen Elizabeth II, Bill Clinton and Thabo Mbeki, sang at Nelson Mandela's 85th birthday, and collaborated with Bono, Miriam Makeba, Hugh Masekela, Angélique Kidjo and Queen. She is married to Dr. Mandlalele Mhinga and has raised five children.

Her second career may outlast the first. As Goodwill Ambassador for the Roll Back Malaria Partnership, Champion for the Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria, and a UN Millennium Development Goals Envoy for Africa, she has spent decades lobbying presidents and parliaments for bed nets, medicines and health funding. In 2006 she founded the Princess of Africa Foundation to anchor that advocacy, and she served as the first ambassador of the Nelson Mandela Children's Fund.

## Historical Context

Chaka Chaka rose to stardom in 1980s apartheid South Africa, when Black artists faced systemic barriers and censorship, and songs like "I Cry for Freedom" and "Motherland" carried liberation-era resonance across the continent. After 1994 she became one of democratic South Africa's most visible cultural ambassadors, then leveraged that standing into global health diplomacy at a time when malaria still killed an African child roughly every minute.

## What She Fought For

She fought first for joy under a regime built to deny it, and then for lives. Her advocacy targets malaria eradication, HIV/AIDS awareness through Mandela's 46664 campaign, children's welfare, adult literacy and women's economic empowerment. Her consistent argument is that health is a right, not a privilege of geography, and that African artists owe their platforms to the communities that built them.

## Major Achievements

- Breakthrough albums "I'm Burning Up" (1986), "Sangoma" and "Thank You Mr. DJ" (1987) that made her a star across Africa.
- Graduated from Trinity College, London with a degree in Speech and Drama (1997).
- Founded the Princess of Africa Foundation (2006) to drive health advocacy across the continent.
- First African woman awarded the World Economic Forum Crystal Award (2012).
- BET Global Good Award (2017).
- Awarded South Africa's Order of Ikhamanga and appointed Honorary Colonel in the South African Air Force.

## Her Impact Today

Yvonne Chaka Chaka is one of Africa's most enduring musical icons and among its most effective celebrity health advocates: a rare figure equally at home on a festival stage and in a Global Fund replenishment meeting. Named among the world's most influential women, she showed a generation of African artists that fame can be converted into bed nets, clinics and classrooms, and that a girl from Dobsonville can sing her way onto the world's most powerful stages without ever leaving her continent behind.
