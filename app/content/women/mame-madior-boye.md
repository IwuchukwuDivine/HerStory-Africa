## Biography

Mame Madior Boye was born on 7 December 1940 in Saint-Louis, then the capital of colonial Senegal, into a family steeped in law: her father was a court clerk and bailiff, and three of her brothers became lawyers. She graduated from the Lycée Faidherbe, enrolled in 1963 at the University of Dakar's Faculty of Legal and Economic Sciences, and completed magistrates' training in France in 1969. She then climbed Senegal's judicial ladder: deputy public prosecutor, judge and First Vice-President of the Dakar Regional Court, and chamber president at the Court of Appeal. Along the way she founded the Association of Senegalese Women Lawyers in 1975 and served as vice-president of the International Federation of Women Lawyers from 1978 to 1998, before moving into banking as a senior director at CBAO.

After Abdoulaye Wade's historic 2000 election ended forty years of Socialist Party rule, she became Minister of Justice, and on 3 March 2001 Wade appointed her Prime Minister, the first woman ever to head a government of Senegal. A non-career politician drawn from the bench, she led a cabinet that included five women, then a record for the country. Her government fell on 4 November 2002 in the aftermath of the MV Joola ferry catastrophe of September 2002, which killed more than 1,800 people, a toll greater than the Titanic. A French judge issued an arrest warrant against her over the disaster in 2008; the Paris Court of Appeal annulled it in 2009.

In 2004 the African Union named her its Special Representative for the Protection of Civilians in Armed Conflict, extending her career of legal service to the continent's war zones. A Muslim, divorced, with two children, she identifies plainly as a feminist.

## Historical Context

Senegal in 2000 lived its first democratic "alternance." Appointing a female technocrat Prime Minister signalled modernisation in a country with strong religious brotherhoods and low female representation in politics. The Joola sinking became the defining tragedy of the era and brought down her government, but the precedent she set outlived the crisis that ended her tenure.

## What She Fought For

Opening the magistrature and the bar to women; competent, law-governed public administration over patronage politics; and, at the African Union, meaningful protection for civilians caught in Africa's wars.

## Major Achievements

- Founded and became first president of the Association of Senegalese Women Lawyers (1975).
- Vice-President of the International Federation of Women Lawyers (1978–1998).
- Named Minister of Justice in Senegal's first alternance government (2000).
- First female Prime Minister of Senegal (3 March 2001 to 4 November 2002).
- Formed a government with five women ministers, then a record for Senegal (2001).
- Appointed AU Special Representative for the Protection of Civilians in Armed Conflict (2004).

## Her Impact Today

Boye proved a woman could hold Senegal's second office, paving the way for Aminata Touré's premiership in 2013 and the era of Senegal's gender-parity law. Her career remains the template of the technocrat-jurist stateswoman in Francophone Africa, and the Joola accountability debate she was swept into still shapes Senegalese public-safety politics. She showed that the path to power for African women need not run through party machines: competence, patiently accumulated, can be its own constituency.
