## Biography

Asha-Rose Mtengeti Migiro was born on 9 July 1956 in Songea, southern Tanzania. Educated at Weruweru and Korogwe secondary schools, she earned her LLB and LLM from the University of Dar es Salaam and a doctorate in law from the University of Konstanz in Germany in 1992. She taught law at the University of Dar es Salaam from 1981 to 2000, heading first the Department of Constitutional and Administrative Law and then the Department of Civil and Criminal Law, building one of the most distinguished legal-academic careers of her generation in East Africa.

Entering government, she served as Minister of Community Development, Gender and Children from 2000 to 2006. In January 2006 President Jakaya Kikwete named her Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, the first woman to hold the post since independence in 1961. Barely a year later, in January 2007, newly installed UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon announced her as Deputy Secretary-General of the United Nations. Taking office in early February 2007, she became only the third person ever, the first African, and the first African woman to hold the UN's number-two job, where she championed development, the Millennium Development Goals and Africa's priorities until mid-2012.

Her service did not stop there. In July 2012 she was appointed the UN Secretary-General's Special Envoy for HIV/AIDS in Africa. At home she served as Tanzania's Minister of Justice and Constitutional Affairs from 2014 to 2015, then as High Commissioner to the United Kingdom from 2016 to 2023, and was Chancellor of the Open University of Tanzania from 2011 to 2016. She is married to Professor Cleophas Migiro and has two daughters.

## Historical Context

Tanzania, independent since 1961 and shaped by Julius Nyerere's ujamaa socialism, transitioned to multiparty democracy in the 1990s and became a diplomatic anchor of East Africa. Migiro's rise coincided with the global push, from Beijing 1995 to the Millennium Development Goals, to bring women into the highest levels of governance. Ban Ki-moon's deliberate choice of an African woman as his deputy signalled that Africa's development agenda would sit at the very top of the UN Secretariat.

## What She Fought For

Gender equality in politics and law; Africa's development agenda and the Millennium Development Goals at the United Nations; access to HIV/AIDS treatment across the continent; and legal and constitutional reform in Tanzania. Her career is a sustained argument that African women belong not merely in the room where global decisions are made, but in the chair.

## Major Achievements

- Doctorate in law from the University of Konstanz (1992), following two decades teaching law at the University of Dar es Salaam.
- Minister of Community Development, Gender and Children (2000–2006).
- First female Foreign Minister of Tanzania since independence (2006).
- Deputy Secretary-General of the United Nations (2007–2012), the first African woman ever in the role.
- UN Special Envoy for HIV/AIDS in Africa (2012).
- Tanzania's Minister of Justice and Constitutional Affairs (2014–2015) and High Commissioner to the United Kingdom (2016–2023).

## Her Impact Today

Migiro proved that African women could hold the very top tier of global governance, and she is cited across East Africa as a role model for women in law and diplomacy. Her UN tenure strengthened the Secretariat's development and Africa portfolio, and her decades of service, as professor, minister, diplomat and chancellor, trace a blueprint for public life that young Tanzanian women now follow in growing numbers.
