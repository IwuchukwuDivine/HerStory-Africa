## Biography

Muthoni Kirima was born around 1930 in Nyeri, in the Kenyan highlands, into a world where the best land had been taken by British settlers and Africans worked it as labourers. She took the Mau Mau oath in 1952. A year later, after colonial soldiers seized and beat her to force information about her husband, she made a decision few women made: she went into the forest to fight.

## The Turning Point

She began by carrying food, medicine and ammunition to the fighters hiding in the Aberdare and Mount Kenya forests. But her marksmanship and nerve moved her from the supply lines to the front, and she rose to become a **Field Marshal**, the only woman in the entire movement to hold that rank. She spent roughly a decade in the forest, separated from her husband, losing a pregnancy to the hardship of that life. When the fighting ended she made a vow that became her signature: she would not cut her hair until she saw real independence, not the version handed to politicians and loyalists while the fighters were left with nothing.

## Historical Context

The Mau Mau uprising of the 1950s was an armed revolt against British land theft and settler rule, met with one of the most brutal counter-insurgencies of the late colonial era, a machinery of detention camps, torture and mass displacement. Women were central to it as fighters, oath-keepers and intelligence networks, and then written out of the story once Kenya became independent in 1963. Muthoni lived that betrayal, spending much of her later life in poverty even as her country celebrated the freedom she had fought for.

## Her Impact Today

For about seventy years she kept her vow, her matted dreadlocks becoming one of Kenya's most recognisable emblems of the independence struggle. In April 2022 they were finally cut, by Mama Ngina Kenyatta, in a ceremony Muthoni said marked Kenya's full independence. She died in 2023, aged around ninety-two, honoured at last as a national heroine and a living reminder that the fight for freedom was carried, in part, by women the history books had left out.
