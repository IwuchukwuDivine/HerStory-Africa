## Biography

Sylvia Rosila Tamale was born in 1962 in Uganda. She earned her Bachelor of Laws with honours from Makerere University, a Diploma in Legal Practice from the Law Development Centre in Kampala in 1990 (top of her class), a Master of Laws from Harvard Law School, and a PhD in sociology and feminist studies from the University of Minnesota in 1997, supported by a Fulbright-MacArthur scholarship. She joined Makerere's Faculty of Law as a teaching assistant in September 1985 and served the university for 37 years until her retirement in April 2022.

From 2004 to 2008 she was Dean of the Faculty of Law, the first woman ever to hold that post at Makerere, and she founded the university's Law, Gender and Sexuality Research Centre. Her 1999 book "When Hens Begin to Crow: Gender and Parliamentary Politics in Uganda" became a landmark of East African feminist scholarship; she went on to edit the influential "African Sexualities: A Reader" (2011) and to write "Decolonization and Afro-Feminism" (2020). She led the drive that made Makerere the first Ugandan institution with a sexual harassment policy, adopted in 2006, and in 2018 chaired the committee investigating sexual harassment at the university, presenting findings drawn from 234 interviews.

On 28 October 2016 she became the first female lecturer to deliver a professorial inaugural lecture at Makerere, titled "Nudity, Protests and the Law." Her honours include the Inspirational African Feminist Award (2011), the Akina Mama wa Afrika Award for human rights activism (2004), and election as an international honorary member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. She is married to fellow Makerere law professor Joe Oloka-Onyango.

## Historical Context

Tamale rose through a post-Amin Uganda where universities were male-dominated and public discussion of gender and sexuality was taboo. Her defence of LGBT rights came amid escalating state homophobia that culminated in Uganda's anti-homosexuality bills, making her scholarship not an abstract exercise but a public stand taken at real personal cost.

## What She Fights For

Legal equality for women and sexual minorities; an African feminism rooted in decolonised knowledge rather than imported frameworks; and institutions free of sexual exploitation. Her core argument is that African law must be rebuilt from African realities, and that feminism on the continent is not a Western import but an African inheritance.

## Major Achievements

- PhD in sociology and feminist studies, University of Minnesota (1997).
- Published "When Hens Begin to Crow: Gender and Parliamentary Politics in Uganda" (1999).
- First female Dean of Law at Makerere University (2004–2008).
- Edited "African Sexualities: A Reader" (2011) and received the Inspirational African Feminist Award the same year.
- First woman to deliver a professorial inaugural lecture at Makerere, "Nudity, Protests and the Law" (2016).
- Published "Decolonization and Afro-Feminism" (2020).

## Her Impact Today

Tamale's students and her research centre have shaped a generation of African feminist lawyers, and "Decolonization and Afro-Feminism" is now a core text in African studies and legal curricula worldwide. Makerere's sexual harassment policy became a model for the region. She showed that a scholar could stand against both patriarchy and state persecution from within an African university, and win the argument on African terms.
