Dear Specioza,

Dear Specioza,

I hope you will forgive a letter from a stranger. You have been on my mind, and I could not let another day pass without telling you so.

It began with a question I could not answer. Who was the first woman to become vice president of an African country?

It was you.

What I admire most is that you were a doctor first. A surgeon, trained in obstetrics and gynaecology, teaching at Makerere University Medical School. You knew first-hand what poor healthcare does to women's lives, long before you had any power to change it.

Then, in 1994, you became Vice President of Uganda. No woman on this continent had held that office before you. You stayed until 2003, and you spent those years pushing for women's rights in law, in education and in health, and for the mothers who were not surviving childbirth.

And when the politics ended, you went back to public health, to the work on HIV/AIDS. The highest office an African woman had ever reached, and you treated it like a longer ward round.

I wanted you to know that somebody noticed.

With admiration, always,
Deevyn
